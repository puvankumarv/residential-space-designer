# Zoning and privacy

## Zones
Typical zones are `sleep`, `study`, `dressing`, `storage`, `leisure` (den, play, sofa) and `entry`. Give each zone an owner in shared rooms. Keep the floor centre open as a shared activity area [8, 67, 68].

## Divider hierarchy (lightest to heaviest)
1. Sheer or blackout curtain on a ceiling track [many]
2. Arched opening or frame [15, 52, 89, 97]
3. Curved low partition [65, 75]
4. Frosted, fluted or tempered glass panel, which keeps the light [8, 86, 94]
5. Divider cabinet, storage on both faces [10, 75, 94]
6. Gypsum or fire-resistant board partition [3, 5, 7, 73]
7. Partition plus a hinged or sliding door [54]

Pick the lightest divider that solves the complaint. Older siblings of different sexes get level 4 or higher with a door [54, 86].

## Fairness in shared rooms
- Mirror the layouts: same wardrobe volume, desk width and storage for each child [5, 51, 75, 94].
- If one child gets the upper bed, compensate the other, for example with the window desk or the larger wardrobe. Say so in the brief.

## Privacy and delight for kids and teens
- An enclosed den or reading nook, often with a hidden or secret door [1, 5, 7, 41, 69, 78, 82, 92].
- A curtain or sliding window on the upper bed [k9P027Hktzg, 10].
- A desk wide enough for a parent to sit alongside a younger child [20, 40, 44].

## Couples
- His/her storage split by drawer or side; a split desk for WFH and vanity [30, 74, 97].
- A walk-in closet behind a false wall at the bed head [39, 45, 70, 76, 83].

## Reveal order (camera path)
Order the `reveal_path` stops like this: entry view → the main transformation (beds) → each occupant's zone → storage details → services or lighting → a final wide shot. Each stop gets a camera position and target in the layout JSON.
