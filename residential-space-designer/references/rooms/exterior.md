# Exterior, site and outdoor spaces

The skill designs interiors, but how a home sits on its plot decides light, privacy and arrival. Use this module when the request covers a whole house, a renovation with façade or garden work, or outdoor rooms. Planning rules (setbacks, coverage, height, parking counts) come from the **local authority**, so always ask for them or flag them as unknown.

## Master decision: how the house meets the street
| Decision | Options | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| **Where the car goes** | A front porch or carport; a half-basement; **outside the plot**, so the front becomes a garden walk | [HT-I1, I2, I4] |
| **How people arrive** | **Separate pedestrian and vehicle entries**; a small gate leading to a path, a tulsi or planter, steps and a framed door | [HT-I2, W5] |
| **Privacy from the street** | A higher compound wall; a **raised plinth** (about 1–1.5 m) to lift living spaces above street eye level and noise; planting screens | [HT-I2, I4] |
| **Where the open space goes** | Front garden as a social space; a **central courtyard**; a side courtyard to open a wall with privacy; a back or side garden | [HT-I1, I2, I3, W5] |

## Outdoor rooms
| Space | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Verandah / sit-out / porch** | Shade, evenings, a swing, guests | Deep enough for chairs (≥ 1.8 m [std]); faces the garden or street [HT-I1, I7] |
| **Courtyard** | Light, air, family gathering | Skylight or open to the sky; rooms around it with large doors; floor seating and a swing in Indian homes [HT-I2, W3, W5] |
| **Balcony** | Coffee, plants, drying | Shutters on sunny sides; a slide-out deck for a barbecue in small flats; irrigation for plants [HT-W1, W2] |
| **Terrace** | Drying, gatherings, pool, solar, future rooms | Covered + open parts; can shade parking below; plan structure for a future floor or pool [HT-I2, I4, W3] |
| **Deck** | Outdoor living for tiny and small homes | Doubles the usable living area in fair weather [HT-W6] |
| **Garden** | Views from every window, slow living | Put planting where the main windows look [HT-I5, I4] |

## Façade and climate
- **Orientation:** big glazing towards the winter sun; shading or double glazing on the west [HT-I1, W5].
- **Roof form:** sloping roofs shed monsoon rain and recall local houses; flat roofs give usable terraces [HT-I1, I2].
- **Openings:** large openings on the free sides of dense plots; corner windows; bay windows into double heights [HT-I3].
- **Materials:** local and durable (brick, stone, wood, terrazzo); keep existing structures where possible [HT-I2, I3, W5].

## Safety (see `safety.md`)
- Guards on terraces, balconies and raised plinths; never place climbable furniture against a low guard.
- Pool and water-feature safety for homes with children (barriers, covers).
- Structural checks before adding terraces, pools, heavy planters or cantilevers.

## Deliverable
For whole-house requests, add a **site sketch** to the home plan: the plot with north arrow, road side, car and pedestrian entries, open spaces (front, courtyard, back), and which rooms face them.
