# Home office

Source tags are explained in `../sources.md`. Desks are also checked in bedrooms and studios.

## Master decision: desk orientation
- Put the window **to the side** of the screen, not behind it (glare on the screen) or behind you (glare and a backlit face on video calls) [std].
- Keep a clean, closable background for video calls: a cabinet wall or a curtain.
- If the office shares a bedroom or living room, use a cabinet desk that closes or a curtain so work can be hidden after hours.

## Storage moves
Overhead cabinets above the desk [DI 3, 48]; a pegboard or pinboard; a mobile drawer unit under the desk; a closet office (desk inside a wardrobe with bi-fold doors).

## Patterns from room videos
| Pattern | How | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Command position** | See the door, solid wall behind; if not possible, angle the desk across a corner for a partial view | [YT-O1] |
| **Never back to the door** | Movement behind you breaks focus | [YT-O1] |
| **Separate work from home** | In open plans, a tall bookshelf, curtain or screen defines the zone, and closed storage puts work away | [YT-O1] |
| **Something solid behind** | No wall behind the desk? A low bookcase, console or plants | [YT-O1] |
| **Three layers of light** | Daylight (avoid windowless rooms), general light and a task lamp; cooler light for focus, warmer for evenings | [YT-O1] |
| **Converting rooms** | A two-desk office that becomes a guest room; a lounge chair that becomes an office chair, with the TV revealed at night | [YT-S1], [YT-S2] |
