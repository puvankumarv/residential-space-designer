# Designing for Western homes

Load this for homes in Europe, North America and Australia/NZ, or households living that way. The patterns come from whole-home tours (`[HT-W…]`, see `interior-design-research/home_tours.md`). Code numbers in `safety.md` follow the US IRC; for other countries, use the local code.

## Intake: extra questions
1. House, apartment, heritage building or tiny house? Any **original features** worth keeping (floors, brick, stairs)?
2. Do you **work from home**? Do you need a door, or just a zone?
3. Kids: ages, and whether you want to **see them while cooking**.
4. Entertaining style: casual kitchen gatherings, formal dinners, outdoor barbecues?
5. Climate: heating-dominated or cooling-dominated? Where does the sun come from?

## Patterns that make small Western homes work
| Pattern | How | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **A real entrance, even when tiny** | A drop zone for keys, mail and seasonal shoes; off-season shoes stored elsewhere; a shoes-off genkan step if wanted | [HT-W1, W2] [DI 4, 93] |
| **Open kitchen with sight lines** | Cook while watching kids play or guests talk; a breakfast bar facing the garden or patio | [HT-W2, W3] |
| **Hide most belongings** | Full-height closed joinery; aim for about 80% hidden and 20% on display | [HT-W1] |
| **Transformable living** | Sliding screens over the TV wall; ceiling-track curtains to carve a gym or guest room; movable modules that become a single bed | [HT-W1, W2] |
| **Kitchen micro-tricks** | Toe-kick (skirting) drawers; short- and long-term pantries; a deep bench with an appliance zone behind the sink; glassware on open shelves in front of windows to keep the light; task lights in front of the cook; a rolling trolley; the bin inside the triangle | [HT-W1] |
| **Stair as storage** | Drawers and cupboards in every tread, an open bookshelf side; kids' clothes drawers at the bottom in loft homes | [HT-W3, W6] |
| **Cave bedrooms, open living** | Small cosy bedrooms so the shared living can be big and bright | [HT-W3] |
| **Swing-free doors** | Sliding doors, curtains for bathrooms in tiny homes, short doors for kids' rooms | [HT-W3, W6] |
| **Keep original fabric** | Restore floors, vaulted brick ceilings and brick walls; don't strip everything | [HT-W2, W3, W5] |
| **Plan for growth** | Roof terraces and lofts that become rooms later; kids' rooms that become studies | [HT-W3, W6] |

## Larger family houses
- **Separate pedestrian and vehicle arrivals**; an entry gallery or foyer that frames the house [HT-W5].
- **Kitchen–dining–family as one informal heart**; a **sunken lounge** for a cosy pocket inside an open plan [HT-W5].
- **Multiple living rooms with distinct moods** (formal, family, pavilion or den) so the whole house gets used [HT-W5].
- **Every room opens to a courtyard or garden** with large sliding doors; orient living spaces to the sun (north in the southern hemisphere, south in the northern) [HT-W5].
- **Sloping sites:** split levels following the slope; the master suite at the top for privacy and views; a casual or wellness level at the bottom (gym, sauna, plunge pool) [HT-W4].
- Typical Western rooms to offer: a **mudroom** (back entry with boots, coats and laundry), a **walk-in closet**, an **ensuite** per main bedroom, a **home office**, a **laundry room**, a **pantry**.

## Tiny houses
Go wider if roads allow (3 m instead of 2.5 m); add a **deck** as an outdoor room; one **sleeping loft per pair of sleepers** that transfers to the kids later; storage in the stairs; toy storage in the kids' loft; curtains instead of doors [HT-W6]. Lofts follow `safety.md` (guardrails, headroom).
