# Designing for Indian homes

Load this when the home is in India or the household follows Indian living patterns. The patterns come from whole-home tours (`[HT-I…]`, see `interior-design-research/home_tours.md`) and the small-space case studies `[DI]`. These are **cultural preferences, not codes**: ask, don't assume. Safety and code rules in `safety.md` still apply. Indian projects should be checked against the National Building Code of India and local bye-laws rather than the US IRC numbers.

## Intake: extra questions
1. Do you follow **Vastu**? Strictly, loosely, or not at all.
2. Do you need a **pooja room**, a pooja unit, or a corner? Who uses it daily?
3. **Cooking style:** daily Indian cooking with tadka and pressure cookers? Do you want a separate **wet/work kitchen or utility**? Is there household help?
4. **Elders** living with you? Any knee or stair issues?
5. Is there **staff** (cook, driver, maid)? Do they need a separate entry, toilet or room?
6. Flat or independent house? Plot size and road side (for example a 30×40 or 60×40 ft east-facing plot).

## Room program typical of Indian homes
| Space | What it does | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Foyer** | Shoes off; a mirror and console are common; a threshold before the living | A shoe cabinet **outside or right inside the door**; seating to put shoes on [DI 4, 93] [HT-I6] |
| **Pooja room / unit** | Daily prayer; sacred and clean | NE if following Vastu; often **raised 15–20 cm**; can pair with a reading or library seat; doors or a jaali screen; not under a staircase or against a toilet wall (preference) [HT-I2, I3, I6] |
| **Sit-out / verandah** | Transition from outside; evenings; guests | A deep, shaded front verandah or porch; a swing is common [HT-I1, I7] |
| **Courtyard / double height** | Light, ventilation, family gathering | A central courtyard with a skylight, or a sunken double-height living the rooms overlook [HT-I2, I3, I5] |
| **Living + dining** | Often one long room | Divide it with a jaali, a jharokha-style screen or a fluted-glass partition rather than a wall [HT-I6, I8] |
| **Kitchen (dry)** | Family cooking, shown to guests | U or L shape with a work triangle; **deep drawers for pressure cookers and kadai**; tall pantry beside the cooktop to hide appliances; dishwasher next to the sink; a ducted chimney [HT-I6] |
| **Wet kitchen / utility / wash area** | Heavy cooking, washing vessels, washing machine, drying | Behind the main kitchen, opening to a duct or balcony; floor drain; tap at low height for vessel washing [HT-I4, I7] |
| **Wash-basin area** | Hand-washing outside the toilets, near dining | A basin counter with a mirror in a passage near dining [HT-I7] |
| **Elders' bedroom** | Grandparents | **Ground floor, no steps**, close to the pooja room, with an attached bath and a sit-out [HT-I1, I4] |
| **Guest bedroom** | Visiting relatives (frequent) | Ground floor, with an attached bath [HT-I2, I4] |
| **Family lounge / TV room** | Upstairs gathering, OTT | Between the kids' rooms, or on the private floor [HT-I1, I2] |
| **Terrace** | Drying, gatherings, solar, future expansion | Covered + open terraces; can shade the parking below [HT-I2] |
| **Staff room / toilet** | Household help | Separate access along the side margin, away from family zones [HT-I1] |

## Vastu: commonly used placements (only when the client wants it)
These are the placements Indian architects used in the tours. Present them as options, and say when they conflict with light, views or the plot.

| Element | Common placement |
|---|---|
| Main entrance | N, E or NE |
| Pooja | NE |
| Kitchen | SE (NW is a common alternative) |
| Master bedroom | SW |
| Children / guest | NW or W |
| Water body, underground sump | NE |
| Heavy masses: stair, lift, service core, store | SW or S |
| Open, light areas: garden, courtyard | N, E, NE |
| Toilets | Avoid NE |

Sources: [HT-I2, I3, I4].

## Climate and light (most Indian cities)
- **Cross-ventilation first:** openings on opposite sides, transoms and ventilators, a stack effect through double-height spaces and skylights [HT-I2, I3].
- **West sun:** double glazing, deep overhangs, verandahs, jaalis or louvres on west façades [HT-I1].
- **Dense plots** with neighbours on three sides: skylights over the living, stair and bathrooms; glass-block ceilings; openings on the free sides [HT-I3, I5].
- **Monsoon:** sloping roofs, covered sit-outs, floor drains in wet areas, waterproofing.
- **Materials seen:** Athangudi tiles, Jaisalmer and Kota stone, terrazzo, teak, exposed brick and concrete, cane [HT-I2, I3, I5, I6, I8].

## Apartments (2/3 BHK flats)
- The **utility balcony** off the kitchen holds the washing machine, drying and vessel washing. Keep it.
- **Lofts above doors and wardrobes** for suitcases and seasonal storage.
- A **pooja unit** in the living or dining (a wall unit with doors) when there's no room for a pooja room.
- Split the living into a sit-out and a dining zone with a screen [HT-I8]. One bedroom can double as a kids' activity room or guest room.
- Grills and mosquito mesh on windows; check society rules before changing façades or balconies.

## Multigenerational living
Plan **privacy by floor**: elders and guests downstairs, the couple and children upstairs, or a married son's suite on its own floor [HT-I2]. Put a shared gathering space at the junction so generations still meet.
