# House on a sloping site: family of five who love hosting

## Questions I'd ask first (5)
1. Where is the site (country and climate), and how do you live? For example shoes-off, elders visiting or living with you, household staff, any religious or cultural rooms such as a prayer room or Vastu.
2. Which way does the slope fall, and by roughly how much? Which side is the street, and where are the sun and the best view? A survey or plot sketch would be ideal.
3. Who are the five: the kids' ages, and does anyone work from home? Do you want a ground-floor bedroom for older relatives?
4. What does hosting look like for you: dinners for 10–12, big summer barbecues, kids' parties, or guests staying overnight (how often, how many)?
5. Plot size, number of cars, budget, and anything the local authority has already told you (height limit, setbacks, coverage)?

**Assumptions so I can carry on** (please correct):
- A temperate **northern-hemisphere** site (for example the UK, northern Europe or the northern US), Western way of living, no cultural rooms needed. If you're in India, the room list changes (pooja room, dry/wet kitchen, utility, elders on the ground floor) and I'd redo the program.
- A plot of about 20 × 40 m. The **street is on the uphill north side**, and the land falls **about 3.0 m** to a south-facing garden with the view. So sun, garden and view are all on the same downhill side.
- Two parents (one works from home), kids aged about 13, 10 and 6. Dinners for 10–12 often, summer garden parties, guests staying a few times a year. Two cars.
- All sizes are **estimated**. Planning rules (setbacks, height, coverage, parking) are unknown and must come from your local authority.

## Home plan

### Master decision for the whole home: split levels that follow the slope
Three half-levels step down the hill, 1.5 m apart, each meeting the ground at its own level. That gives arrival at the street, the kids' wing in the middle and the hosting heart at the garden, with the master suite on top.
**Rejected:**
- *Flatten the site with one big platform:* heavy retaining walls and spoil removal, and you lose the natural walk-out to the garden.
- *Two full storeys with a basement dug into the hill:* the basement rooms get no daylight or escape windows on the uphill side, and you need deep tanking.
- *Everything on the street level with the garden a storey below:* guests would carry food down a full flight to every barbecue, so hosting fails.

Separate pedestrian and vehicle entries. Big glazing and the terrace face south to the winter sun, with eaves or a pergola for summer shade. Glazing to the west is kept small or shaded.

### What changes, cost flags, order
- **Structure:** retaining walls on the uphill side of each level, with tanking and land drainage (specialist + structural engineer). Each level steps the foundations. This is the biggest cost driver, so ask for the soil report early.
- **Drainage:** the garden level is the lowest point, so foul drainage may need a pump if the sewer is at the street. Confirm the invert levels before fixing the kitchen and guest suite positions.
- **Pool:** barrier fencing and a self-closing gate are non-negotiable with a 6-year-old. Engineer the pool on the downhill side.
- **Order to design:** (1) site survey, levels and planning check → (2) structure and drainage strategy → (3) **hosting heart** and **stair core** (detailed below) → (4) kitchen scullery and terrace → (5) kids' wing bedrooms → (6) master suite → (7) mudroom/entry.

I've detailed the two spaces that make or break hosting on a slope: the garden-level heart and the stair that connects the levels. I'll do the kids' rooms, master suite and mudroom next if you agree.

## Room A: garden-level hosting heart (`20-a.json`)

### 1. Design brief
**Room:** `living_dining` with an open kitchen, 10.0 × 7.0 × 3.0 m (estimated). Sliding glass wall on the south (garden) side; the north wall is retained earth with the stair opening and the scullery door; kitchen window on the east.
**Occupants:** two adults (both cook), three kids, up to 12 guests.
**Diagnosis (new build, so the risks to design out):** guests crowding the cook's work zone; a host with their back to the party; the route from the stair to the garden cutting through the sofas or the kitchen; dirty dishes on show; nowhere for guests to get their own drinks.
**Master decision (seating focus and circulation): conversation-first.** Two sofas facing each other at the west end, with the fireplace and hidden TV between them. A 3.0 m dining table in the middle. An island kitchen at the east end where the sink faces the room. The main route runs from the stair straight to the garden **between** the lounge and the dining table, beside both groups, never through them.
**Kitchen shape: island + back run, with a scullery behind.** Two-cook aisle 1.25 m.
**Rejected:**
- *Sofa facing a TV wall:* it turns the hosting room into a cinema, and guests sit in a row instead of talking. The TV stays, behind panels.
- *L-sofa in a corner:* fine for a family, but it seats 5–6, not a party, and it closes the lounge to the dining.
- *Galley or L kitchen against the wall:* the host faces the wall all evening.
- *Hob on the island:* fumes and spatter towards guests, and kids at the stools beside a hot hob. So the hob is on the back run, the sink on the island.
- *Sunken lounge:* a lovely cosy pocket, but a step down in a room full of guests carrying plates (and a 6-year-old) is a trip hazard. You can add it later if you want it.

## Room B: split-level stair, garden (0.0) to kids' wing (+1.5) (`20-b.json`)

### 1. Design brief
**Room:** `stairs`, 2.4 × 4.0 m stair hall open to the level above (void height 4.2 m, estimated). Opens south into the hosting heart; the landing is the kids' wing floor at +1.5.
**Users:** everyone daily, the 6-year-old, guests carrying plates.
**Diagnosis:** on a sloping site, the stairs *are* the house's corridors. Steep, winding or uneven flights make hosting across levels miserable and dangerous for kids.
**Master decision (route widths): short, straight, identical half-flights.** 8 risers of 0.1875 m and 7 treads of 0.28 m, 1.1 m clear width, a glass balustrade on the open side and a glass guard on the landing edge. Every half-flight in the house repeats these exact dimensions.
**Rejected:**
- *Winders or a spiral to save space:* uneven going, bad with trays and small kids.
- *Ramps:* at 1.5 m of rise a gentle ramp is over 18 m long. Consider one outside along the garden path instead if accessibility matters.
- *Open risers:* a 6-year-old's foot or a dropped toy goes through; closed risers are also better for guard-opening rules.
