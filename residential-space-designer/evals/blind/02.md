# Twins, 9, room 3.4 × 3.4 m, ceiling 2.8 m: ending the bunk-bed fight

## Questions I'd ask first (5)
1. Which walls have the door and the window? Which way does the door swing? Is there a radiator, an AC unit or a beam?
2. What is the fight actually about: do both want the top bunk, or does one hate being underneath? Would they accept *no* top bunk if each got an identical den?
3. Are they the same sex? (At 9 this changes little now, but it tells me whether to plan for a divider in a few years.)
4. Does each need their own desk for homework, or do they mostly study elsewhere?
5. Where is the home, which floor is this room on, and do you know which walls are load-bearing? Any budget or style preference (warm wood, colour per child)?

**Assumptions so I can carry on** (please correct):
- Door centred on the south wall, opening in; one 1.2 m window centred on the north wall, sill 0.9 m. The room is on the first floor, with about a 3 m drop outside. Door and window positions are **estimated**.
- Same-sex or mixed twins, both 9, both need a desk. The fight is over who gets the top.
- All walls treated as load-bearing (so no niches cut into them). No particular regional or cultural needs. Mid budget, warm wood with a colour per child.

## 1. Design brief
**Room:** `kids_bedroom`, 3.4 × 3.4 × 2.8 m (11.6 m², square). Door S (centre, opens in); window N (centre, egress). Fixed services unknown.
**Occupants:** twins A and B, both 9.
**Diagnosis** (from the bedroom checklist):
- *Classic bunk:* a stacked bunk makes one child the "winner" and one the "loser" every night. The lower bunk is cramped and neither has privacy.
- *Shared kids' room:* fighting, unequal space, no privacy, so the fix is mirrored equal zones plus a personal den.
- Desks and wardrobes have to share a square room without blocking each other's doors.

**Master decision: SIDE-BY-SIDE PLATFORM beds (sleep-strategy branch "side-by-side raised platforms", pattern E).** Two identical platform beds against the west and east walls (mattress top 0.55 m) with wheeled drawers under. Each has a ceiling-track curtain on its open side that turns the bed into a private den. The window wall is split exactly in half into two desks. The room is 3.4 m wide, above the 3.2 m this pattern needs. It also removes the thing they fight over: there is no "top" any more.

**Rejected:**
- *Classic stacked bunk:* it's the cause of the fight. It also needs a ceiling ≥ 2.9 m for comfortable headroom on both beds; yours is 2.8 m.
- *Offset L-loft (pattern D, the usual fit for this exact room):* it still has one upper bed, so the argument just moves. You'd need a rota plus compensation for the other child.
- *Upper bed on back-to-back wardrobes (pattern F):* needs a room at least 4 m long; this one is square.
- *Two lofts side by side, each over its own desk:* fair and fun, and 2.8 m does fit a loft over a desk (1.5 + 0.25 + 0.84 ≈ 2.6 m). But it means two steel frames, two sets of storage steps and window guards, roughly double the cost, and the steps eat the shared floor. It's the upgrade if both kids insist on sleeping up high.

**Fairness:** everything is mirrored: same bed, same 0.85 m wardrobe, same full-height bookcase, same 1.1 m desk, the same half of the window. Nobody gets compensated because nobody gets less.

## 6. Safety notes
**Checker output:**

- **The two warnings are deliberate and handled:** the desks sit under an upper-floor window, so a child could climb onto them and reach the opening. Fit restrictors that limit the opening to about 100 mm but that an adult can release for escape. The window stays the room's **egress opening** (sill 0.9 m ≤ 1.12 m [IRC]), and nothing tall stands in front of it. If the room is on the ground floor these warnings go away.
- Mattress top 0.55 m is below the 0.60 m raised-bed threshold [EN747], so no guardrails, ladders or age limit apply. Each bed has a free 0.6 × 0.9 m+ strip along its open side to get in ✔.
- Door swing clear ✔; sliding wardrobe doors and desk chairs each keep 0.6 m clear ✔ (small-room minimum [NP] [IDE]).
- Anchor every tall unit to the wall (tip-over). Round all corners. Use flame-retardant curtain fabric, and fix the curtain tracks into the ceiling joists.
- **[std] values to confirm:** 0.6 m clearance in front of the desks (common practice), and the adjustable child-desk range.
- Storage: 0.85 m of wardrobe per child plus a bookcase and three drawers each, above the 0.6–0.8 m per child target.
- These numbers follow US/EU guidance. Check your local building code.

## 7. Reveal script
1. **Entry:** One bunk bed, two kids, a fight every night about who gets the top. Now there is no top.
2. **Beds:** Two identical platform beds, one on each wall, same height, same size. Nothing left to argue about.
3. **Den:** Pull the curtain and each bed becomes a private den with its own reading light and shelf.
4. **Drawers:** Three wheeled drawers under each bed for toys and spare bedding, no lift-up lids.
5. **Wardrobes:** Matching floor-to-ceiling wardrobes with sliding doors, so they open even with a chair pulled out.
6. **Desks:** The window is shared exactly down the middle: one 1.1 m desk each, same daylight.
7. **Wide shot:** And the middle of the room, 1.5 m wide, is shared floor for play.
