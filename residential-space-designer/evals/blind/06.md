# Brother (14) and sister (11), shared 3.2 × 4.2 m room

A 14-year-old boy and an 11-year-old girl is a case where my design rules say a curtain or bookcase isn't enough. Each of them needs a space with a real wall and a door. So the plan below splits the room into two small private bedrooms. First, a few questions:

**Questions (up to 5):**
1. What is the ceiling height? (Lofted beds need about 2.6 m or more.)
2. Which wall has the door, and which has the window? Roughly how wide is the window, and does it have two opening panes or one?
3. Where is the home, and is there anything about how you live that I should plan for? For example a country or climate, religious or cultural needs, or shoes-off.
4. What causes the most friction now: getting changed, noise, sleep times, mess, screens? And does either child need anything special (instrument, gaming, sport kit, a big drawing desk)?
5. Do you know which walls are load-bearing, and is there an AC unit or a radiator anywhere?

**Assumptions so I can go ahead** (please correct any of them):
- Ceiling 2.7 m.
- Door near the middle of one short wall (S, 0.8 m, opens inward).
- One 1.8 m window in the middle of the opposite short wall (N), sill 0.9 m, with two opening panes.
- All walls treated as load-bearing. I don't cut into any wall.
- No special cultural needs.

---

## 1. Design brief

**Room:** kids_bedroom, 3.2 × 4.2 × 2.7 m (13.4 m²). Door on S (offset 1.2 m), window on N (offset 0.7 m, 1.8 m wide). Fixed services unknown. Ceiling, door and window are assumed.

**Occupants:** brother, 14 (teen); sister, 11. Both need to sleep, study, get dressed, and have privacy.

**Diagnosis:**
- **Teen + sibling of the other sex.** Privacy, especially for getting changed, is the deciding need. Open zoning isn't enough.
- **Shared kids' room: fairness.** Both halves must be equal, or the split itself causes fights.
- **No room for a desk** if two beds sit on the floor of 3.2 m. Two beds plus two desks plus two wardrobes don't fit at floor level with the clearances they need.

**Master decision (how they sleep):** I'm using the *"Teen + sibling of the other sex → full partition with a door"* branch. The room becomes **two mirrored private cells, each 1.55 × 3.1 m**. Each cell has a **loft bed (mattress top 1.85 m)** with the **desk and wardrobe underneath** and **storage step-ladder drawers** at the entrance end. A shared 1.0 m lobby inside the room door holds a drop-zone cabinet for each child. The two cells are separated by a fire-board stud partition with acoustic infill (divider level 6), and each has its own sliding door (level 7).

**Rejected:**
- *Side-by-side platforms with a divider cabinet* (the usual pick for rooms ≥ 3.2 m wide): a cabinet with no door isn't enough privacy for a 14-year-old and an 11-year-old of different sexes.
- *Offset L-bunk:* both beds in one open space, so there's no privacy at all.
- *Upper bed on back-to-back wardrobes:* the wardrobe spine gives sight-line privacy but no door, and the child on the window side gets all the light.
- *Classic stacked bunk:* poor headroom and no privacy. That's the main complaint the room has to fix.
- *Floor beds inside each cell:* a 1.55 m wide cell can't hold a bed, a desk and a wardrobe on the floor. Lofting the bed is what makes room for the desk.

**Fairness:** the plan is an exact mirror. Same cell size, same 1.1 m desk at the window, same 0.9 m wardrobe, same 1.1 m lobby cabinet, same steps and drawers. Each cell gets half the window.

**Key trade-offs:**
- Each cell is small (about 4.8 m²). The children get privacy in exchange for open floor.
- The partition has to meet the window frame in the middle, so the window needs two panes that each open on their own. If your window is a single pane, the split doesn't work as drawn. We'd need a second window, or the cells would have to be arranged differently.
- The wardrobes under the lofts are 1.55 m tall: short hanging plus drawers. Long items go in the lobby cabinets.

**Assumptions to confirm:** ceiling, door and window positions; that each half of the window is big enough to escape through under your local code; the [std] values below.

## 2. Zone plan

| Zone | Purpose | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| entry (lobby, 3.2 × 1.0 m) | Drop zone, two cabinets, two cell doors | shared |
| sleep_b / study_b / dress_b | Loft bed, desk at the window, wardrobe, steps | brother (west cell) |
| sleep_s / study_s / dress_s | Mirror of the above | sister (east cell) |

Each cell keeps a 0.6 m clear strip from its door to its desk. That strip is the cell's walkway and the space in front of the desk and wardrobe.

## 6. Safety notes

- Checker: **0 errors, 3 warnings** (explained above).
- Loft headroom 0.85 m above the mattress (recommended ≥ 0.84 [RET]). Clear height under the loft for the desk is 1.6 m (≥ 1.5 m, a [std] value).
- Both children are well over the minimum age of 6 for an upper bed [CPSC].
- Guardrails on both long sides, 0.18 m above the mattress [EN747/CPSC]. Keep the mattress thin.
- Each cell reaches its own pane of the window without passing through the other's door. Checked: each partitioned area has egress [IRC].
- The privacy latches must open from outside. Parents need to be able to get in.
- **[std] values:** 1.5 m clear under a loft at a desk; step-ladder sizes (a 0.45 m deep footprint is steeper than the stair-style rule of rise ≤ 0.22 / tread ≥ 0.22, so treat it as a ladder with deep flat treads and a handhold).
- **Needs a professional:** loft frame and anchoring, the partitions (fire and acoustic rating), electrics. Check your local codes, including the minimum size of each window pane used for escape.
