# 3BHK flat, Pune: daily Indian cooking and a maid twice a day

A few questions first (answer whatever you can):

1. Can you share the **floor plan**, or the carpet area and rough room sizes? Which floor is it on, and which way does it face?
2. **Who lives here?** Any elders, children (ages), anyone working from home, frequent guests?
3. **What does the maid do** on each visit: vessels, sweeping and mopping, clothes, chopping or cooking? Does she need to use a toilet?
4. Do you follow **Vastu**, and do you need a **pooja unit or corner**?
5. **Kitchen and utility:** piped gas (MNGL) or a cylinder? How big is the utility balcony? Are you open to moving the sink or other plumbing points (that's a major cost)?

Assumptions so I can give you a complete plan:
- A typical Pune 3BHK of about 1,050 sq ft carpet. The kitchen is about 2.7 × 3.3 m with a 2.7 × 1.2 m utility balcony off it. Ceiling 2.85 m. Mid-rise floor.
- Couple + one child + one parent (or a guest room). One person works from home some days.
- **Maid** comes morning and evening: vessels, sweeping and mopping, clothes, some chopping. She comes in by the main door (flats rarely have a service entry).
- A pooja unit in the living or dining area, loose Vastu. Piped gas. Plumbing stays where it is.

### Key whole-home decisions
- **Split dry and wet work** [HT-I4, I7]. The dry kitchen is for cooking. Heavy vessels, grinding and laundry go out to the utility balcony. Your utility balcony already exists, so keep it, don't enclose it into the kitchen.
- **The maid's route stays in the public zone:** foyer → dining → kitchen → utility. Inside the kitchen she walks a clear lane on the pantry side, never past the hob.
- **Foyer:** shoe cabinet with a bench and a slipper gap. One shelf or hook set for the maid [DI 4, 93].
- **Pooja unit** on a dining wall, with doors, not sharing a wall with a toilet (preference).
- **Lofts** over the bedroom doors and wardrobes for suitcases. Grills and mosquito mesh on windows. Check society rules before any façade or balcony change.
- **Pune climate:** hot dry summers and heavy monsoon. Shade west windows, keep cross-ventilation across the living and dining, and slope the utility floor to its drain.

### What changes and cost flags
- **No plumbing moves.** The sink stays on the existing kitchen wall and the washer on the existing utility drain. Moving either is a major cost.
- The new modular kitchen, a sunken vessel-wash bay in the utility (waterproofing redone) and new electrical points are the main works.

### Order of design
1. Kitchen (detailed below). 2. Utility balcony (detailed below). 3. Foyer and pooja unit. 4. Bedroom 3 as kid's room plus WFH. 5. Wardrobes and lofts.

# Room A: Kitchen (`18-a.json`)

## 1. Design brief
**Room:** `kitchen`, 2.7 × 3.3 × 2.85 m (**estimated**). Opening from dining on the S wall (east end), sliding door to the utility on the N wall (east end), window over the N counter.
**Occupants:** family cook (daily Indian cooking: tadka, pressure cooker, rotis), maid twice daily (chopping, rinsing, carrying vessels out).
**Diagnosis** (kitchen checklist, typical for Pune flats):
- One long platform: the cook and the maid collide, and the maid crosses the hob to reach the utility. **Through traffic crosses the work triangle.**
- Vessels pile up in the single kitchen sink, and there's no landing space beside the hob.
- Corner cabinets go unused, there's no place for cookers and kadais, and the chimney isn't ducted.

**Master decision: U-shape.** Hob on the **west** wall, sink under the **north** window, fridge and a tall pantry on the **east** wall. That leaves a **1.5 m aisle**, above the 1.22 m needed for two cooks [NKBA]. The maid's lane to the utility door runs along the east (pantry) side, away from the flame. **Vessel washing moves to the utility balcony.**

Work triangle: hob–sink 1.70 m, sink–fridge 1.73 m, fridge–hob 2.16 m. Total 5.6 m, inside 1.2–2.7 m per leg and 7.9 m total [NKBA].

**Rejected:**
- *Single straight platform:* this is the current problem. No triangle, one person at a time.
- *L-shape (hob and sink only):* no room for a fridge and tall pantry without blocking the utility door.
- *Parallel galley:* puts traffic along the hob side, and the utility door sits at the end of the cook's run.
- *Open kitchen to dining:* daily tadka fumes and oil drift into the living. Keep the opening narrow; a sliding glass shutter is optional.

**Key trade-offs:** the sink stays on the existing plumbing wall (the N window wall). The fridge sits at the east wall, about 1.7 m from the sink, which is acceptable.
**Assumptions to confirm:** room size, plumbing and gas points, chimney duct route (shaft or façade; check society rules).

# Room B: Utility balcony (`18-b.json`)

## 1. Design brief
**Room:** `balcony` (utility), 2.7 × 1.2 m (**estimated**), off the kitchen through a sliding door at the east end. Open north side with a 1.0 m parapet and grill. Assumed drop about 15 m (mid-rise floor).
**Occupants:** maid (squats at a low tap to wash vessels, does laundry), family.
**Diagnosis:** washing vessels at the kitchen sink floods the counter. Clothes dry on the grill. Mops and buckets lie on the floor. There's no low tap.
**Master decision (wet-utility stack):** washer at the **west end** on the existing drain. A **sunken vessel-wash bay** (nahani) with a **low tap** and floor drain just inside the door. A shallow broom and cleaning cupboard on the kitchen wall. A **ceiling pulley drying rack** [DI 26, 38] [HT-I4, I7].
**Rejected:**
- *Raised utility sink at counter height:* the maid washes heavy vessels squatting, as is usual. A raised sink only suits plates.
- *Laundry tower (stacked washer and dryer):* Pune drying is mostly by air. A dryer isn't worth the space.
- *Enclosing the balcony into the kitchen:* this loses the wet zone and ventilation, and usually needs society permission.
