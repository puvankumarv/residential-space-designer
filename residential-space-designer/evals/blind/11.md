# Narrow kitchen, 2.4 × 3.2 m: two cooks without the collisions

A 2.4 m wide room is exactly the galley width band, so the shape is not the problem. The problem is that both of you need the same metre of floor at the same time. The fix is to give each of you a station, stagger the stations so you never stand back to back, and win back a few centimetres of aisle.

## A few questions (I'll carry on with assumptions so you have a full design now)

1. Where are the door and the window? Which wall has the sink drain, and where is the gas or hob point?
2. Ceiling height?
3. When you cook together, who does what? For example, one at the hob while the other washes and chops?
4. Where is the home, and how do you cook? Daily heavy Indian cooking (pressure cookers, kadai, a chimney, maybe a separate wash area) or lighter Western cooking? Do you want a dishwasher?
5. Budget level: new cabinets throughout, or reuse the carcasses and change the fronts and layout?

**Assumptions used below (please correct me):** door centred on the short south wall; window (sill 1.0 m) on the far north wall; ceiling 2.7 m; sink drain on the east wall near the door; gas/hob point on the west wall towards the far end; all walls treated as load-bearing; one person mostly at the hob, the other on fridge, washing, chopping and serving.

## 1. Design brief

**Room:** kitchen, 2.4 × 3.2 × 2.7 m (ceiling height assumed). Door: south wall, centred. Window: north wall. Services: sink on the east wall, hob on the west wall (assumed, not confirmed).
**Occupants:** two adults who cook at the same time.

**Diagnosis**
- **Two people sharing one work point.** In a standard 0.6 + 0.6 m galley the aisle is 1.20 m, just under the 1.22 m two-cook guideline [NKBA]. If the sink is directly opposite the hob as well, you stand back to back in the same spot.
- **Through traffic crosses the work triangle.** Whoever fetches from the fridge or carries plates out walks behind the cook.
- **Doors swinging into the aisle.** Hinged base-cabinet doors, the dishwasher door and the room door all block the aisle while open.
- **The hob is probably near the doorway.** That is a safety issue: the rule is never right beside a door.

**Master decision: a two-station galley with staggered work points and an asymmetric depth.**
- **Station 1, the helper, at the door end:** fridge, sink and the main chopping counter. Everything the "go-between" person needs is within a step of the door.
- **Station 2, the cook, at the far end:** the hob with landing space on both sides, a tall pantry and a serving counter. The cook stays put and nobody passes behind them.
- **The east run is 0.55 m deep** (slimline carcasses, sink with a rear-set drain), which makes the **aisle 1.25 m**. That meets the 1.22 m two-cook guideline [NKBA]. It is 1.20 m at the fridge, at the door end where nobody is cooking.
- **Work triangle:** fridge to sink 1.87 m, sink to hob 2.39 m, hob to fridge 2.05 m. Total 6.3 m, within the 7.9 m limit and 1.2–2.7 m per leg [NKBA]. The checker confirms it.

**Rejected alternatives**
- *U-shape (counter across the window end):* the base of the U becomes a dead end, so the person at the far end is trapped behind the other. It also blocks the window and adds a blind corner.
- *One-wall kitchen plus a mobile trolley:* a 2.4 m room is wider than the one-wall band (≤ 2 m). You'd lose half the storage and both of you would still stand on the same line.
- *Standard galley with 0.6 m runs:* 1.20 m aisle, and the stations land opposite each other. This is the layout that causes the bumping now.
- *Island or peninsula:* needs at least 1.07 m on every working side. Impossible in 2.4 m.

**Key trade-offs**
- The 0.55 m east run needs slimline base units, and a standard 0.6 m dishwasher won't fit flush on that side. Options: a slimline 45 cm dishwasher in the west prep run, or no dishwasher (common in Indian kitchens).
- If your sink drain is actually on the west wall, I'd mirror the plan. Moving the drain or the gas point is plumber and gas-fitter work and the biggest cost here.

House rule that goes with it: the cook's back is a no-pass zone. Whoever is at the helper station hands things across, and doesn't walk behind the hob.
