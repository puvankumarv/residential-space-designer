Below is a plan for how the whole flat works first, then detailed designs for the three rooms that carry the most daily load: the living-dining room, your bedroom and your child's room. Kitchen, bathroom and balcony get guidance in the home plan, and I can detail them next.

## A few questions (I'll carry on with assumptions so you have a full plan now)

## Master decision: room allocation

- **Bedroom 1 (bigger) goes to the couple** because it has to hold the most activities: a queen bed with a side for each of you, 2.3 m of wardrobe (≥ 1.0 m per adult) and a real desk.
- **Bedroom 2 goes to your child,** using height: a mid-sleeper bed with a play den under it frees the floor for play and a homework desk.
- **The two WFH desks go in two different rooms.** Desk A is in bedroom 1; desk B is a closet office in the living room. You can both take calls with a door between you. On non-WFH days, both close away.

**Rejected alternatives**
- *Child in the bigger room:* bedroom 2 (3 × 3 m) can't fit a queen bed with two usable sides plus a 2 m wardrobe plus a desk, so the parents would lose their desk.
- *Both desks side by side in bedroom 1:* two simultaneous calls in one room don't work, and the bedroom becomes an office.
- *Working at the dining table:* the skill's rule is that a corner with a door or a closable cabinet beats the dining table. Otherwise laptops and cables cover the table at every meal.
- *Converting the balcony into an office:* it's the only outdoor space and probably the laundry spot. Enclosing it also needs structural and permission checks.
