# A niche behind the bed (3.0 × 3.4 m bedroom, ceiling 2.7 m, double bed on the north wall)

**Short answer: I won't cut into the north wall until we know it isn't load-bearing.** You don't know which walls carry load, so I treat all of them as load-bearing. My safety rules don't allow cutting niches or openings into a load-bearing wall. A wall behind a bed is often a party wall or an outside wall, which very often carries load.

You can still have the niche without cutting into that wall. My plan builds a slim new wall 20 cm in front of the north wall and recesses the niches into that. It's non-structural, needs no permission to cut, and can go in now. Once the wall is confirmed non-structural, I've also drawn the cut-in version.

**Questions (up to 5):**
1. Is the north wall an outside wall, or is there another flat or room behind it? Roughly how thick is it? Measure at the door frame or a window reveal.
2. When you knock on it, does it sound hollow (stud or drywall) or solid (brick or concrete)? Do you have the building drawings, or can the building office or landlord tell you if it's structural?
3. Is anything in or behind that wall: pipes (a bathroom or kitchen on the other side), the electrical board, or sockets back-to-back with a neighbour's?
4. Which walls have the door and the window?
5. What should the niche do: replace nightstands (phone, glasses, lamp), display things, hold books? And would you give up 20 cm of floor at the foot of the bed for it?

**What I've assumed so I can go ahead:** all walls load-bearing; door on the south wall near the east corner (0.8 m, opens in); window on the east wall (1.2 m, sill 0.9 m); one existing double bed (1.4 × 2.0 mattress, frame 1.5 × 2.05); the niches replace nightstands and add a display shelf.

---

## 1. Design brief

**Room:** bedroom, 3.0 × 3.4 × 2.7 m. Double bed against N. Door assumed on S, window assumed on E. Load-bearing status of the walls unknown, so all are treated as load-bearing. Not estimated (dimensions given), but door and window positions are assumed.

**Occupants:** one adult or a couple (not stated).

**Diagnosis:**
- **Two nightstands taking up space.** The usual fix is wall niches or headboard shelves instead. That's exactly what's being asked.
- **Structural status unknown,** which blocks the usual "cut a niche" move.

**Master decision (bedroom: how the bed and its wall work).** The bed stays a floor double against N. The change is a **built-out headboard wall**: gypsum board on metal studs, 0.2 m in front of wall N, full width and height. It has three recessed niches: two lit bedside niches (0.5 × 0.45 m, 0.15 m deep) that replace the nightstands, and one long display niche (1.6 × 0.35 m) above the upholstered headboard. The bed moves forward 0.2 m.

**Rejected:**
- *Cut niches into wall N now:* not allowed while its structure is unknown. Cutting a load-bearing wall can crack or weaken it.
- *Wall-hung floating shelves:* no structural risk, but they don't give the flush built-in look, and corners stick out by the pillow.
- *A headboard unit with shelves (furniture):* a good cheaper fallback with no building work. It's less integrated and the outlets show.

**Key trade-offs:** the room loses 0.2 m of depth. The walkway at the foot of the bed goes from about 1.35 m to 1.15 m, still well above the 0.6 m small-room minimum. In return there's no structural risk, and the cavity hides new cables for the outlets and lights.

**Assumptions to confirm:** door and window positions; that the bed frame is about 1.5 × 2.05 m; nothing in wall N that the new wall's fixings would hit.

### Once the north wall is confirmed NON-load-bearing (`08-b.json`)

If an engineer, the building drawings or the building office confirms wall N is a non-structural partition, and nothing runs inside it where we'd cut, the niches can go straight into it. That way you don't lose the 20 cm. The niche depth is limited by the wall: a 100–115 mm partition allows only about 80–100 mm, still enough for a phone and glasses. Put a small lintel over the long niche. If the wall turns out to be load-bearing, the built-out wall above stays the answer.

## 6. Safety notes

- Checker on `08.json`: **0 errors, 0 warnings**. On `08-b.json`: 0 errors, 0 warnings, valid only once wall N is confirmed non-load-bearing.
- **Never cut niches or openings into a load-bearing wall.** If unknown, assume it is. The checker blocks a niche in a wall of unknown structure (see the demo).
- Before fixing the new wall, scan wall N for pipes and cables.
- Electrics inside the new wall: a qualified electrician, installed before the boards go on.
- Check your local codes.
