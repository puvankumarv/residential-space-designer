# Scenario 04: couple, 3.0 × 3.2 m bedroom, vanity + work from home

## Questions I'd ask (5)
1. How high is the ceiling? Which wall has the door, which has the window, and how wide is the window?
2. What bed size do you have now (double 1.4 m or queen 1.5–1.6 m)? Would you go smaller to make the room work?
3. His work: how many hours a day, how many video calls, and how many monitors? Does he need to shut the work away in the evening?
4. How much hanging space does each of you need, and does she need a long-hang section (dresses, coats)?
5. Where do you live, and is there anything about how you live that I should design around (for example Vastu, a pooja shelf, shoes off)? Any idea which walls are load-bearing?

**Assumptions so the design is complete:** ceiling 2.7 m; the door is on the west wall near the south corner and opens inwards; the window is on the north wall (1.0 m wide, 0.9 m sill) and is the escape window; queen bed 1.5 × 2.0 m; one or two monitors and daily calls; a long-hang section for her; all walls treated as load-bearing, so no niches; no particular cultural requirements.

## 1. Design brief
**Room:** bedroom, 3.0 × 3.2 × 2.7 m (9.6 m²). Door W, window N (both estimated). Ceiling estimated.
**Occupants:** a couple. She needs a vanity; he works from home.
**Diagnosis**
- *Couple: one wardrobe for two; desk vs vanity conflict.* Usual fix: split the storage by person and give them a split desk.
- *Two nightstands taking wardrobe space.* Usual fix: headboard shelves instead.
- A bed in the middle of 9.6 m² leaves no wall long enough for a desk with room for a chair.
- WFH in a bedroom needs a background that closes for video calls, and work that can be put away at night.

**Master decision (how they sleep): a queen storage platform pushed into the SE corner, head on the south wall, with his and hers drawers. A split L-desk takes the NW corner: her vanity under the window, his WFH desk on the west wall** (couple branch, Pattern H).
- The vanity faces the window, so her face gets daylight while she does her make-up.
- His desk is at right angles to the window. The window sits *to the side* of his screen, so there's no glare and his face isn't backlit on calls (home-office rule). His camera looks east, towards the bed, so a ceiling-track curtain runs between desk and bed. Drawn, it gives a plain call background and hides the bed. A closed cabinet above the desk keeps work out of sight after hours.

**Rejected**
- Bed centred with both sides free: 1.5 + 2 × 0.6 = 2.7 m of the 3.0 m width, which leaves no wall for a desk.
- Bed running across the room (head on the west wall): 2.0 m bed + 0.7 m desk + 0.6 m chair = 3.3 m, more than the 3.0 m width.
- One shared desk in the window (Pattern H as written): the window would sit behind his screen. That causes glare and a backlit face on calls.
- Walk-in closet behind a false wall at the bed head: costs about 0.6 m of length that this room doesn't have.
- Raised bed (mattress top above 0.6 m): adults climbing up every night, with no gain here.

**Key trade-offs**
- **The sleeper on the east side gets in from the foot of the bed** (the checker flags this). The 0.6 m strip in front of the wardrobe is their way in. Give that side to whoever gets up less at night.
- His chair zone (0.75 m) is also the west-side sleeper's bedside strip. If he works late, his partner is going to bed right next to the chair.
- Wardrobe: 1.4 m, against a target of 2.0 m for two adults. The shortfall is made up with the platform drawers (about 0.6 m³), the overhead cabinet above the bed and the closed cabinet above the desk.
