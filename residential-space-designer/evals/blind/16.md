# A loft and stair in a 5 m duplex volume

A few questions first. Answer whatever you can:

1. How big is the double-height space (width × depth), and where would you like the loft: along the back, over the kitchen or over the entrance? What is it for: bedroom, study, guest, storage?
2. Is the 5 m clear to a flat RCC slab, or are there beams? Who built the house, and do you have the structural drawings?
3. Where are the entrance door, the windows (especially any tall double-height glazing) and the other doors in that space?
4. Who will use the stair daily: elders, small children, someone carrying laundry or bedding?
5. Where is the home (city and country)? Stair and loft rules, and whether a loft counts towards built-up area or FSI, depend on the local code.

Assumptions so I can give you a full design now:
- The double-height living space is about 4.2 × 6.5 m, 5.0 m floor to ceiling. The entrance door is on the west wall, there's tall glazing high on the south wall and a normal window on the north wall.
- The loft is a **habitable** study or guest space that you use every day. That makes the stair the **main route** between levels, so it must meet stair rules, not ladder rules.
- The home is in India ("duplex"). The checker uses US IRC stair numbers. I note where to confirm against NBC 2016 and your local bye-laws.

## 1. Design brief

**Room:** `stairs` (double-height living with a new loft), 4.2 × 6.5 × 5.0 m. All dimensions are **estimated** until you confirm them. Entry W, tall window S (sill 2.9 m), window N under the loft. All walls treated as load-bearing.
**Occupants:** household adults. The stair is a daily route, and people carry things up it.
**Diagnosis:**
- About half of the 5 m volume is unused air. Floor area is short, and the ceiling height is the asset.
- Any stair here must climb about 2.6 m to a loft floor that leaves standing height both below and above: 2.4 m clear below + 0.2 m structure + 2.4 m clear above = 5.0 m.
- A straight flight needs about 3.4 m of run plus a landing at the foot. Along a 6.5 m room, that leaves a loft only about 2.1 m deep.

**Master decision (route and stair form): a quarter-turn (L) stair with a full 1.0 × 1.0 m landing, in the SE corner of the open half of the room. It rises to a 3.9 m deep loft over the back (north) of the room.**
- 14 risers × 0.186 m = 2.60 m; 12 treads × 0.26 m. Riser ≤ 0.196 m and tread ≥ 0.254 m [IRC-S]. Width 1.0 m ≥ 0.914 m [IRC-S].
- Flight 1: 7 risers along the south wall up to the corner landing at 1.30 m. Flight 2: 7 risers along the east wall to the loft edge.
- Both flights sit under the open 5 m volume, so headroom is never less than 2.4 m. That's at the loft arrival, against ≥ 2.03 m [IRC].
- Under flight 2 there's room for a 1.56 m bank of pull-out drawers (shoes, vacuum, bedding) [DI 13, 43, 65].

**Rejected:**
- *Straight flight along the east wall:* it works, but the bottom landing plus 3.4 m of run leaves only about a 2.1 m deep loft. About half the floor area is lost.
- *Spiral stair:* compact, but it doesn't meet the minimum tread depth across its width, and carrying a mattress up one is miserable. Fine only for an occasional storage loft.
- *Alternating-tread stair or ship's ladder:* furniture-grade access. Not acceptable as the main route to a habitable loft.
- *Winders instead of the landing:* saves about 0.3 m but gives uneven treads at the turn. With elders or children, the flat landing is safer.
- *Stair under the loft edge:* the loft slab underside at 2.4 m would cut headroom to well under 2.03 m.

**Key trade-offs:** the stair takes the SE corner and the low south wall. The tall south glazing (sill 2.9 m) stays clear above it. The loft adds floor area, which may count towards built-up area or FSI, so check before building.
**Assumptions to confirm:** room size and openings, slab and beam layout, local stair code (NBC 2016 / bye-laws), loft use.

## 6. Safety notes

- Checker: **0 errors, 0 warnings.** Riser 0.186 ≤ 0.196 m, tread 0.26 ≥ 0.254 m, width 1.0 ≥ 0.914 m, headroom 2.4 ≥ 2.03 m. Foot clearance 0.9 m and under-stair drawer clearance 0.6 m are clear.
- **Not checked by the script, set by design:** risers and treads within 9.5 mm of each other [IRC-S], handrail 0.86–0.97 m and the 2.03 m headroom (IRC R311.7, verify locally), guard openings < 0.10 m (0.15 m at the stair-side triangle) [IRC-S], loft guard height (commonly 0.91–1.07 m [std]; I used 1.0 m).
- **India:** confirm against NBC 2016 and your municipal bye-laws (riser, tread, width, guard height). The stricter value wins.
- Never let climbable furniture stand against the loft guard. Put a safety gate at the stair head if there are toddlers.
- Structure is **not DIY**: an engineer must confirm the loft frame, its bearing and the anchors.
