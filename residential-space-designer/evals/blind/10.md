# 4 m² micro-room where you can't lie flat

Not being able to stretch out to sleep is the first thing to fix. In a room this small, the usual fix is a **wall bed that folds up during the day**. That gives you a full-length bed at night and a desk and floor space by day. Something drastic like pushing the wall out (an extension past the outside wall) is only ever a job for a structural engineer with planning permission. I won't suggest it as DIY.

**Questions (up to 5):**
1. What are the exact inside dimensions (wall to wall, both ways) and the ceiling height? Most important: **is any wall at least 2.05 m long?** A 2.0 m bed needs that.
2. Which walls have the door and the window? Which way does the door open? Is there a radiator, pipes or an AC unit?
3. Who sleeps here, and what else happens in the room: working, getting dressed, guests?
4. Do you own or rent? Can things be fixed to the walls and floor? (A wall bed has to be anchored.)
5. Where is the home? The minimum size for a bedroom differs by country, and I'll flag it if this room is under it.

**What I've assumed so I can go ahead** (dimensions estimated, marked `"estimated": true`):
- Room 1.7 × 2.35 m (4.0 m²), ceiling 2.6 m.
- One adult who also works here.
- Door 0.7 m in the south wall, opening inward.
- Window 1.0 m on the east wall, sill 0.9 m.
- All walls treated as load-bearing; walls can be drilled for fixings.

If **no wall reaches 2.05 m**, a flat full-length bed can't fit on its own. The options become a shorter 1.9 m mattress if the longest wall is about 1.95 m, a bed that runs past the door opening with the door rehung to open outward, or, only with an engineer, a structural change. Tell me the real numbers and I'll redo the layout.

## 1. Design brief

**Room:** bedroom, 1.7 × 2.35 × 2.6 m (**estimated**). Door on S (offset 0.95 m, 0.7 m wide, opens inward), window on E (offset 0.75 m, 1.0 m, sill 0.9 m). Fixed services unknown.

**Occupants:** one adult. Needs to sleep flat, work and store clothes.

**Diagnosis:**
- **Micro-unit (≤ 4 m²): can't lie flat.** The usual fix is a fold-up or pull-out bed, or an extension designed by an engineer.
- **No room for a desk** while the bed is down.
- Storage is almost certainly at floor level only, and the walls above aren't used.

**Master decision (how you sleep): a side-tilt (horizontal) fold-up wall bed** along the 2.35 m west wall. At night: a full **0.9 × 2.0 m single bed**, lying flat. By day: it tilts up into a housing 0.4 m deep and 1.1 m high, and a **desk folds out from its underside** (it stays level). Every wall above goes to the ceiling as storage. A full-height wardrobe with mirrored sliding doors makes the room look bigger.

**Rejected:**
- *Vertical (end-tilt) wall bed:* it folds out 2.05 m from the wall, but the room is only 1.7 m wide.
- *Pull-out sofa-bed from drawers:* it needs a 0.9 m pull-out zone plus the drawer box, and it eats the same floor as the desk. Workable, but it gives you no daytime desk.
- *Loft bed:* the 2.6 m ceiling could take one, but climbing to bed in a 4 m² room, with the bed next to the window and right over the door, is worse than folding a bed down. Keep it as a backup if you'd rather not fold a bed daily.
- *Structural extension (cantilever beyond the outside wall):* only with an engineer and planning permission. Not a DIY option.

**Key trade-offs:** you fold the bed every morning and evening (about 10 seconds with gas struts). At night the desk is gone, and you have 0.75 m beside the bed to the wardrobe. The wardrobe is only 0.75 m wide, so the overhead cabinets and the cabinet over the door make up the rest.

**Assumptions to confirm:** every dimension and opening; that the walls can take the wall-bed fixings; and that a room this small counts as a legal bedroom where you live.

## 6. Safety notes

- Checker: **0 errors, 0 warnings** in both the `night` and `day` states.
- The bed is a floor bed (mattress top 0.45 m), so no guardrails are needed. Night state: 0.75 m free beside the bed to get in (≥ 0.6 m).
- The desk is only usable with the bed up. Never fold the bed with things on the desk unless the mechanism is designed for it.
- The window stays clear for escape; nothing tall in front of it.
- **Wall beds must be anchored** exactly as the manufacturer specifies. A falling wall bed is a serious hazard.
- **A 4 m² room may be below the legal minimum for a bedroom** in many places, and it still needs an opening window. Check your local code.
