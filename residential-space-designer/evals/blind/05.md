# Scenario 05: teen girl, 2.8 × 2.9 m, ceiling 2.4 m, wants a gaming hideout

## Questions I'd ask (5)
1. How old is she, and roughly how tall?
2. Which wall has the door, which has the window, and how wide is the window? Any AC unit, radiator or beam?
3. How does she game: console and TV, a PC with a desk and monitor, or handheld? Does she play alone or with friends sitting in?
4. Does she still need a separate desk for homework, and how much hanging space does she need?
5. Where do you live, and anything about how your home works that I should design around? Which walls are load-bearing (if you know)?

**Assumptions so the design is complete:** she's 14 and of average height; the door is on the south wall near the east corner and opens inwards; the window is on the north wall (1.0 m wide, 0.9 m sill) and is the escape window; she games on a console with a wall-mounted screen in the den, sitting on floor cushions or a beanbag, alone or with one friend; she needs a homework desk and a 1.4 m+ wardrobe; all walls treated as load-bearing (no niches).

## 1. Design brief
**Room:** kids_bedroom, 2.8 × 2.9 × 2.4 m (8.1 m²). Door S, window N (both estimated).
**Occupant:** a teenage girl, about 14. She wants a private hideout to game in, plus somewhere to sleep, study and store clothes.
**Diagnosis**
- 8.1 m² with a low ceiling: a floor bed, a desk and a wardrobe fill it, with no space left that is *hers*.
- *Privacy and delight for teens:* the usual fix is an enclosed den, often with a hidden door.
- The low ceiling (2.4 m) rules out a loft over a desk (that needs about 2.6 m).

**Master decision (how she sleeps): a LOW loft (mattress top at 1.42 m) over an enclosed gaming den 1.2 m high, with the den's door hidden in wardrobe-style panels; the desk goes in the window.**
This is the "gaming den under the bed" variant of the loft branch. The dimensions table allows a loft over a den at a ceiling of 2.4–2.6 m: 1.2 m den + 0.22 m bed + 0.98 m headroom = 2.4 m.
**Rejected**
- Half-height bed (the decision tree's default for a ceiling below 2.6 m): it fits easily, but only 0.55 m under the bed is no hideout, and she would have to give up the den.
- Loft over a desk: needs about 2.6 m of ceiling, and you have 2.4 m.
- Platform bed + a curtained corner: privacy only by curtain, and it uses about 2 m² of floor.
- Pattern B as written: it assumes a ceiling of 2.5 m or more. At 2.4 m the den is for *sitting only*, no standing, which is fine for a cushion den.

**Key trade-offs:** you can't stand in the den, only sit (1.2 m clear height; the minimum is 1.0 m). The loft's 0.98 m headroom is above the 0.84 m recommended, but she'll sit up carefully. Every part of this design is built in, so it's harder to change as she grows.

## 6. Safety notes
- Checker: **0 errors, 0 warnings** (output above).
- Raised bed: mattress top at 1.42 m, guardrail 0.18 m above the mattress [EN747] [CPSC], headroom 0.98 m (0.84 m recommended) [RET].
- **Den:** 1.2 m clear height, against a 1.0 m minimum for a sitting-only den **[std]**. **The den door must open from inside with a push** (no key, no lock), and the den needs ventilation: a louvred panel plus a gap at the loft. Never seal it, since consoles get hot. Use a low-heat LED strip.
- The loft isn't next to the window, so no window guard is needed. The escape window stays clear; only the 0.74 m desk sits under it.
- Pattern B lists a ceiling of 2.5 m or more; this design uses 2.4 m by keeping the den to sitting only. Flag it if she wants to stand inside.
- Check your local building codes.
