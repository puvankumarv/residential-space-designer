# Living room 3.5 × 4.5 m: getting people out of the TV's way

A few questions so I can get this exactly right (answer whatever you can):

1. Where are the doors (front door, doors to the kitchen, bedrooms or balcony) and the windows? Which wall is each on, roughly how far from a corner?
2. How big is the TV, and does it have to stay on its current wall (cable or antenna point, wall mount)?
3. How many people sit here on a normal evening, and for guests? Does anyone eat in this room?
4. Where is the home, and how do you live? For example shoes off at the door, a pooja unit, floor seating, an AC unit.
5. Ceiling height, and do you know which walls are load-bearing?

So you get a complete plan now, I've made these assumptions. Tell me where they're wrong and I'll redo it:
- Front door on the short south wall near the east corner. A second door (to the rest of the home) on the north wall, also near the east corner. Window on the north wall towards the west.
- Today the TV is on the west wall and the sofa is against the east wall, so the door-to-door route runs between them. That's why people walk in front of the screen.
- Ceiling 2.75 m, all walls treated as load-bearing, a household of 3–4 adults, 43–55 inch TV.

## 1. Design brief

**Room:** `living`, 3.5 × 4.5 × 2.75 m. Front door S (east end), inner door N (east end), window N (west end). Dimensions given; door and window positions assumed.
**Occupants:** household of 3–4, plus occasional guests.
**Diagnosis** (living-room checklist):
- **People walk between the sofa and the TV.** Both doors sit on the east side, so the natural line between them runs straight through the viewing zone.
- The sofa is against a wall, so the route has nowhere to go except in front of it.
- Loose furniture and no storage; shoes and keys end up on the sofa arm.

**Master decision (seating focus and circulation): the focus is the TV on the long west wall. The sofa floats 0.9 m off the east wall with its back to the route, so the route runs behind the seating, never through it.**
- The east strip (x 2.6–3.5 m) becomes a 0.9 m door-to-door lane, the "ideal" main walkway [BO][IDE].
- Seated eye to screen is about 1.9 m, which suits a 43–55 in TV at 1.5–2.5 × the diagonal [std].
- Coffee table 0.36 m from the sofa front, inside the 0.36–0.46 m range [RET-L].

**Rejected:**
- *Keep the sofa on the east wall, TV on the west wall:* the route still crosses the screen. This is the current problem.
- *L-sofa in a corner:* in 3.5 m of width the L's return arm ends up across the route or blocks a door.
- *TV on a short wall (N or S):* both short walls have doors, so the screen would sit beside a doorway and the lane would still pass in front of it.
- *Two sofas facing each other:* conversation-first layouts need more width. At 3.5 m the lane would disappear.
- *Move a door:* this works too (an inner door moved to the west end), but it's masonry work and possibly structural. Keep it as plan B.

**Key trade-offs:** a floated sofa needs a low or finished back, because it's now seen from the lane. A floor-level power point for the side lamp is optional; I've used the N wall instead.
**Assumptions to confirm:** door and window positions, TV size, ceiling height, load-bearing walls. The 1.5–2.5 × diagonal viewing rule is [std] (common practice, not sourced).
