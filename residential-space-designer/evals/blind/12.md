# Tiny bathroom, 1.7 × 2.5 m: stop the floor flooding

A floor that floods after every shower almost always comes down to three things: nothing holds the water in (no screen, or only a curtain), the floor doesn't fall towards the drain, and the drain is too small or in the wrong place. In 4.25 m² the fix is a proper **wet/dry split**. The shower gets the full width of the far end behind glass, the floor there drops slightly and falls away from you to a linear drain, and everything else stays in a dry front zone.

## A few questions (I'll carry on with assumptions so you have a full design now)

1. Where are the door, the toilet and the floor drain now? Is there a window?
2. What do you have now: a shower tray, a curtain or nothing, a bathtub, or just a floor trap in the middle?
3. Ceiling height? And is a full renovation possible (breaking up the floor to re-lay the falls), or are you hoping for a lighter fix?
4. Who uses it: how many people, and any kids or elderly users (grab bars, a step-free entry)?
5. Where is the home, and do you use a handheld shower, bucket and mug, or a health faucet by the toilet? This changes where the drains and taps go.

**Assumptions used below (please correct me):** door on the short south wall, opening outward (or easy to change to outward); no window, so an extract fan; ceiling 2.4 m; toilet soil stack on the east wall near the door; walls treated as load-bearing; a full renovation of the floor and screen is possible.

## 1. Design brief

**Room:** bathroom, 1.7 × 2.5 × 2.4 m (ceiling assumed). Door: south wall, 0.4 m from the west corner, 0.7 m wide, opening outward. No window (assumed). Toilet stack: east wall (assumed).
**Occupants:** household (adults assumed).

**Diagnosis**
- **Water everywhere after showering.** There's no fixed glass screen, the floor is flat or falls the wrong way, and the drain is a single point drain. The usual fix is a glass wet/dry split plus a linear drain [DI 14, 31].
- **The floor probably isn't waterproofed under the tiles** (if water seeps next door or the grout stays dark). Waterproofing needs two coats and a 48 h flood test before tiling [DI 14, 31].
- **Bulky fixtures standing on the floor** (a pedestal basin, a close-coupled toilet) trap water and dirt in corners. Fix: a floating vanity and a wall-hung toilet so the floor can be squeegeed and mopped [DI 63, 14, 31].
- **Nowhere to put things**, so bottles sit on the floor and the window sill. Fix: a mirror cabinet and an over-toilet cabinet [DI 56].

**Master decision: shower across the far end with a glass wet/dry split.**
- **Wet zone:** the full 1.7 m width × 0.92 m at the north end. A fixed glass panel plus a 0.7 m hinged glass door across the whole width, with seals.
- **Floor:** the wet zone sits about 25 mm lower than the dry floor [std] and falls about 1.5–2 % [std] *away from the screen* to a **linear drain along the back (north) wall**. Water runs away from the dry side instead of towards it.
- **The shower head goes on the back wall**, so the spray hits the drain side, not the door.
- **Dry zone** (front 1.58 m): a slim floating vanity on the west wall, a short-projection wall-hung toilet on the east wall on the existing stack, and a clear 0.8 m path from the door.

**Rejected alternatives**
- *Wet room (whole floor drains, no screen):* the skill keeps this for rooms under about 3 m². At 4.25 m² it would soak the toilet and the towels every time, which is your current problem made official.
- *0.9 × 0.9 m corner shower beside the toilet:* the toilet would sit in a 0.8 m strip with only 0.40 m per side (code minimum 0.38, recommended 0.46), and a corner tray is a smaller target for the water.
- *All fixtures on one wall:* in a 1.7 m room the toilet and basin would face the shower opening, and splash reaches both.
- *Bathtub with a screen:* a 1.7 m tub uses the full width, but it's harder to step into, the screen only covers half, and it takes the same space as a far more generous shower.

**Key trade-offs**
- The vanity is a slim 0.6 × 0.35 m unit. It gives the toilet 0.77 m of clear space in front (0.76 m recommended [NKBA]). A standard 0.45 m-deep vanity would cut that to code-minimum territory.
- A wall-hung toilet needs a concealed cistern frame built out from the east wall. If the soil stack is not on that wall, moving it is the biggest cost item, so I'd mirror the plan rather than move it.

## 6. Safety notes

**Checker output** (`python3 scripts/check_layout.py 12.json --plan`):
```
0 error(s), 0 warning(s)
```
- The door opens outward, so it can't trap someone who falls inside.
- **[std] values, please verify:** the 25 mm wet-zone step-down and the 1.5–2 % falls. Your tiler and plumber should confirm them, and if anyone needs step-free access, use a level threshold with the linear drain at the screen line instead.
- Code values come from IRC/UPC summaries and NKBA guidance (US). Check your local bathroom and electrical codes.
- **Professional work:** waterproofing, plumbing and all electrics. Don't cut a shower niche into a wall unless it is confirmed non-load-bearing. The alternative is a niche built into the boxed-out cistern wall.
- Use only tempered or laminated glass.
