## Room design: kitchen-dining-family room

### 1. Design brief
**Room:** `kitchen`, 4.6 × 8.0 × 2.75 m (estimated). Hall door at the south-east corner (pocket slider); full-width sliding doors to the garden on the north wall; glazed roof strip over the side return on the west. Services: the soil stack stays at the back; the hob goes on the island with a ceiling extractor ducted up through the flat roof.
**Occupants:** two adults (one or two cooking), kids about 7 and 4.
**Diagnosis:**
- The kitchen is in the narrow outrigger, a galley with its back to the house: you can't see the kids from it.
- The kids play in the back reception or the front room, two walls away.
- The side return is a dark, wasted alley.
- Homework happens away from the adults, so there's lots of calling between rooms.
**Master decision: island kitchen.** A back run of tall units and the sink, plus an island carrying the hob, set across the room so the cook faces the dining table, the sofa and the garden.
**Rejected:**
- *Galley in the outrigger (as now):* the cook faces a wall and the kids are out of sight. That's the problem you have.
- *L-shape along the party wall:* the cook still faces the wall for most tasks, and the route to the garden cuts across the triangle.
- *Peninsula:* good sight lines, but in a 4.6 m wide room it blocks the route to the garden doors.
- *Sink on the island, hob on the back wall:* safer with very young children and easier to vent. But you'd have your back to the room while frying, which is exactly when you most want to watch. It's a fair alternative if your youngest is under 3 (see trade-offs).
