# Scenario 01: 10-year-old's room, 2.6 × 3.0 m, ceiling 2.75 m

A 2.75 m ceiling is enough to lift the bed and put the desk underneath. That fixes both problems at once.

## Questions I'd ask (5)
1. Which wall is the door on, which wall is the window on, and how wide is the window? Any AC unit, radiator or beam?
2. Is the wardrobe built in, or can we replace it? Do you know which walls are load-bearing?
3. Is he happy sleeping up high? Any reason not to (sleepwalking, still growing into it)?
4. Where do you live, and is there anything about how your home works that matters (for example Vastu, shoes off, a shared study with a sibling)?
5. Budget level and style: warm wood, plain white, or a colour he picks?

**Assumptions so the design is complete** (please correct me): the door is on the short south wall near the east corner and opens inwards; one window, 1.0 m wide, sits on the north wall with a 0.9 m sill and is the escape window; the existing wardrobe gets replaced; every wall is treated as load-bearing, so no niches are cut; no particular cultural requirements; mid budget in warm wood.

## 1. Design brief
**Room:** kids_bedroom, 2.6 × 3.0 × 2.75 m (7.8 m²). Door S (estimated), window N (estimated). No fixed plumbing or gas.
**Occupant:** one boy, aged 10. Needs: a place to study, storage, room to play.
**Diagnosis**
- *Wardrobe doors can't open because they hit the bed.* Usual fix: sliding doors, or move the wardrobe.
- *No room for a desk.* Usual fix: put the desk under a loft or in the window.
- At 7.8 m², a bed on the floor plus a hinged wardrobe uses up all the floor. There's nowhere left to play.

**Master decision (how he sleeps): a LOFT bed along the 3.0 m west wall, with the desk underneath** (branch: one occupant, 5–9 m², needs desk + storage, ceiling ≥ 2.6). Height check: 1.5 m clear at the desk + 0.25 m platform and mattress + 1.0 m headroom = 2.75 m, which matches the ceiling exactly.
**Rejected**
- Platform bed: still takes about 1.9 m² of floor, so there's still no desk.
- Half-height bed (mattress top about 0.8 m): only needed when the ceiling is below 2.6 m. It wastes the height you have, and you can't sit at a desk under it.
- Loft with a walk-in closet underneath: needs a ceiling of about 2.9 m and 1.9 m of walk-under height.
- Desk in the window plus a bed on the floor: the bed still blocks the wardrobe.

**Key trade-offs:** the desk only gets 1.5 m of clear height above it. That's fine for a 10-year-old sitting down, but he can't stand up under the loft. The bed is high, so making it up is harder for you.
