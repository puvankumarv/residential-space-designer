# Two boys, a small room of about 3 m, and two desks

That's very doable. The trick is to take one bed up off the floor so both desks fit. To get it right I need a few details:

**Questions (up to 5):**
1. Is the room about 3 m in **both** directions, or 3 m one way and something else the other? What is the ceiling height? A tape measure is enough. A photo with the door in it also works, and I can estimate from that.
2. How old are the boys? The upper bed is only safe for a child aged 6 or older.
3. Which wall has the door, and which has the window? Is there a radiator or AC unit?
4. Where is the home, and is there anything about how you live I should plan for? For example a country or climate, shoes-off, or a cultural need.
5. What's the biggest problem now: fights over space, no study spot, mess, sleep?

**What I've assumed so I can go ahead** (all to be confirmed; the size is an estimate, marked `"estimated": true`):
- Room 3.0 × 3.0 m (9 m²), ceiling 2.7 m.
- Boys aged 10 and 8.
- Door near the east end of the south wall, opening inward.
- A 1.0 m window on the east wall, sill 0.9 m.
- All walls treated as load-bearing.

---

## 1. Design brief

**Room:** kids_bedroom, 3.0 × 3.0 × 2.7 m (**estimated**). Door on S (offset 2.1 m), window on E (offset 0.85 m). Fixed services unknown.

**Occupants:** boy_a (10), boy_b (8). Both need to sleep, study and store clothes.

**Diagnosis:**
- **No room for a desk:** two single beds on the floor of a 3 × 3 m room take about 3.8 m² plus the space to get into them. That leaves no room for two desks with chair space.
- **Shared kids' room:** risk of unequal space and squabbles, so the plan must be visibly fair.
- **Classic bunk risk:** the obvious fix (a stacked bunk) gives poor headroom on top and a cramped lower bunk.

**Master decision (how they sleep):** an **offset L-loft** (pattern D, "compact rooms"). The lower bed runs along the west wall. The upper bed (mattress top 1.8 m) turns the corner along the north wall, not overlapping the lower one, over **boy_a's desk and wardrobe**. A fixed ladder and book tower stand at the junction. **boy_b** gets the window desk and a full-height wardrobe. The centre of the floor stays open.

**Rejected:**
- *Classic stacked bunk:* you need a ceiling of about 2.9 m or more for comfortable headroom in both beds. At 2.7 m, one boy sits up into the slats.
- *Side-by-side platforms:* that works from about 3.2 m of width. At 3.0 m two beds plus a divider leave no room for desks.
- *Upper bed on back-to-back wardrobes:* suits long rooms of 4 m or more. This one is square.
- *Two floor beds with fold-down desks:* the desks and beds would fight for the same floor every evening.

**Fairness:** boy_a gets the upper bed, the under-loft desk (1.0 m) and a 1.0 m wardrobe under the loft (short hang). boy_b gets the lower bed, the **window desk** (1.0 m, best daylight), a **full-height** 1.05 m wardrobe and under-bed drawers. So the boy who doesn't get the loft gets the window and the taller wardrobe. Tell them that's the deal. If the younger one wants the top bunk, just swap the owners; nothing else changes.

**Key trade-offs:** the under-loft wardrobe is 1.5 m tall (short hang only), so long items go in boy_b's wardrobe or the overhead cabinet. Access to the loft is a fixed ladder, not storage steps, because there isn't floor for a proper stair.

**Assumptions to confirm:** every dimension and opening above. If the ceiling is under 2.6 m, I'd switch to a half-height bed (mattress top about 0.8 m) with the desk beside it rather than under it.

## 6. Safety notes

- Checker: **0 errors, 0 warnings**.
- Upper headroom 0.9 m above the mattress (recommended ≥ 0.84 m [RET]). Clear height under the loft at the desk is 1.55 m (≥ 1.5 m, a [std] value).
- Upper sleeper must be 6 or older [CPSC]. Assumed 10, so please confirm the ages.
- Guardrails on both long sides; the wall-side rail can be dropped only if the bed is fixed tight to the wall with no gap [CPSC].
- The upper bed isn't next to the window, so no window guard is needed for it. The window desk is climbable, so fit a window restrictor anyway if it's an upper floor.
- Escape window kept clear: nothing tall in front of it.
- **[std]:** 1.5 m clear under a loft at a desk; ladder pitch (check EN 747).
- Rounded edges on desks and steps. Anchor the tower and wardrobes to the wall. Check your local codes.
