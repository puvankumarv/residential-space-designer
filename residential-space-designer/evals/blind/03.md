# Scenario 03: three kids (5, 8, 12) in 4.0 × 3.5 m, ceiling 2.9 m

14 m² with a 2.9 m ceiling is enough for a corner steel loft system: two beds up high, one on the floor, and an open middle.

## Questions I'd ask (5)
1. Which wall is the door on, which wall is the window on, and how wide is the window? Any AC unit, radiator or beam?
2. Are the children boys, girls, or a mix? In particular, is the 12-year-old a different sex from the others? That decides whether they need a real partition with a door.
3. What do you most want to fix: fighting over space, nowhere to do homework, mess, or bedtime clashes (the 12-year-old reading late while the 5-year-old sleeps)?
4. Which walls are load-bearing, and can a steel frame be bolted into the floor and walls? (Rented or owned?)
5. Where do you live, and anything about how your home works that I should design around (for example Vastu, shoes off)? And your budget level?

**Assumptions so the design is complete:** the door is on the south wall near the east corner and opens inwards; the window is on the west wall (1.4 m wide, 0.9 m sill) and is the escape window; all three children are the same sex, or the family is fine with curtains for privacy for now; all walls are treated as load-bearing (no niches); the home is owned, so the frame can be anchored; mid budget.

## 1. Design brief
**Room:** kids_bedroom, 4.0 × 3.5 × 2.9 m (14 m²). Door S (estimated), window W (estimated).
**Occupants:** three children aged 12, 8 and 5.
**Diagnosis** (the usual problems for three children in one room)
- Three floor beds take about 6 m² of floor, so there's no centre left and nowhere to put desks.
- *Shared kids' room: fighting, unequal space, no privacy.* Usual fix: equal zones for each child and a personal den or curtain.
- Different bedtimes, with the oldest awake later.
- The 5-year-old is under the minimum age for a top bunk (6) [CPSC].

**Master decision (how they sleep): an L-shaped steel multi-bed system on columns, along the N and E walls, plus a floor bed for the 5-year-old** (branch: 3–12 children, Pattern G: 14 m² ≥ 12, ceiling 2.9 ≥ 2.8).
- 12-year-old: upper bed along the north wall, mattress top at 1.85 m, headroom 1.05 m. A 2 m desk row underneath has 1.6 m of clear height.
- 8-year-old: upper bed along the east wall, also 1.85 m, over two sliding wardrobes.
- 5-year-old: floor bed by the window, with wheeled drawers, a low drawing desk and their own wardrobe.
- Each upper bed has its own storage box-stairs, so nobody climbs over anyone else.

**Rejected**
- Classic stacked triple or bunk: the ceiling is only just high enough, nobody gets privacy, and the 5-year-old can't sleep up top.
- Three platforms side by side: a 4 m wall can't take three 0.95 m beds plus their access strips and still leave room for desks.
- Wardrobe-supported upper bed (Pattern F): needs a long room of 4 m or more for one bed. It doesn't solve three.
- A partition splitting the room: a room this size can't give both halves a window. Keep it as an option if question 2 says it's needed.

**Fairness (stated plainly):** the two older children each get an upper bed with a curtain, 1.0 m of desk and a 1.0 m wardrobe. The 5-year-old gets no upper bed, but in return gets the window spot, the only floor bed (easy for a parent to sit on), their own desk and a 0.8 m wardrobe with a low rail they can reach. The 12-year-old's wardrobe sits under the 8-year-old's bed. Hand the zones over by colour.
**Key trade-offs:** the desks get 1.6 m of clear height, which is fine sitting down. The upper beds are hard for adults to make. When the 5-year-old turns 6, nothing has to change. When the 12-year-old reaches the teens, add a fabric curtain to the N loft (planned in) or upgrade to a frosted panel.

## 6. Safety notes
- Checker: **0 errors, 0 warnings** (output above).
- Upper beds: mattress top at 1.85 m, guardrail 0.18 m above the mattress [EN747] [CPSC], headroom 1.05 m (0.84 m recommended) [RET]. Upper-bed sleepers are 12 and 8, both 6 or older [CPSC]. **The 5-year-old must not use either upper bed**, including for daytime play on top. Tell the children this rule.
- Neither upper bed is next to the window, so no window guard is needed. The 5-year-old's floor bed under the window is only 0.45 m high. If the drop outside is more than about 1.8 m, fit window restrictors anyway, because a 5-year-old will climb from the bed to the sill.
- **[std]:** 1.5 m minimum clear height at a desk under a loft (1.6 m here); the 0.6 m clear floor in front of the steps and wardrobes is the small-room minimum.
- Rounded corners on every desk and step, especially at the 5-year-old's eye height.
- Check your local building codes.
