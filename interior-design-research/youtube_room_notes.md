## Kitchens

**[YT-K1] Layout first, finishes later.** Most daily annoyances are fixed by the room plan, months before finishes.
- **Fridge at the end of a run, near the way in**, never in the middle: a tall unit splits the worktop into two pieces too short to work on, and people raiding it cross the cook's path. Shopping goes straight in.
- **The kitchen is also a corridor.** Trace every route (garden, utility, bins, table). If one crosses the strip between sink, hob and fridge, the cook stops every time someone passes. Fix it at plan stage by moving the main access, not the units.
- **Doors that fight.** Every appliance door, drawer and cupboard has a swing; draw them all on one sheet. Common clashes: a corner cupboard blocked by the appliance beside it, a bin drawer unreachable while the dishwasher is open.
- **A layout the room can hold.** An island needs roughly 3.6 m of room width before it stops being an obstacle, with about 1.0–1.2 m clear on the working side; under 1 m two people can't pass. A galley under about 2.4 m wide is a poor place to cook. (Agrees with NKBA aisles in `sources.md`.)
- **Light and the view from the sink.** A north-facing kitchen with one window means cooking under electric light; low west sun glares off gloss worktops; a rooflight in the wrong place lights the floor. You stand at the sink longest, so give it a view, not a blank wall.
- **Drains and extraction decide the sink and hob.** The sink needs a drain with enough fall; the hood duct should be short and straight to an outside wall. Check the existing stack and external walls first.
- **Plan for two people.** Ask where the second person stands. Give them a second surface away from hob and sink, with its own worktop and socket; even about 800 mm of return or island end works.

**[YT-K2] Indian kitchens: fire, water, then storage.**
- Fix **fire and water first**: the **longest counter takes the hob** (Indian cooking needs room for the rolling board and everything around it); the site's drain and water point fix the sink. Decide the counter height, and whether the hob is built in or sits on top.
- Save **human energy**: count the trips between washing machine, dishwasher, hob, fridge and storage; keep the sink–hob–fridge triangle tight.
- **"Modular" means modular dimensions**, not more hardware: cabinets in standard widths of **300, 450, 600, 750, 900 mm**, because baskets, cutlery trays, plate racks and rolling shutters only come in those sizes. Odd widths (700, 780 mm) leave dead pockets that collect clutter.
- **List what you store before drawing**: dals and pulses (small quantities, many canisters → shallow drawers sized to the canister count), atta, rice and sugar (bulk → a grain trolley), oils, ladles. Choose a pantry type (pull-out tall unit, glass shelves over drawers, or a stack of tandem drawers) to suit the list and space.
- **Corners**: a swing-out corner unit with round trays suits round kadais and pressure cookers; heavy-duty trays carry cookers.
- **Appliance garage**: a rolling shutter (450 / 600 / 900 mm) with the daily appliances on shelves and **sockets behind**, so nothing is unplugged and moved. List current and future appliances first.

## Bathrooms

**[YT-B1] Layout mistakes and designer swaps.**
- **Don't put the WC beside the head of the bath.** Put the basin between them, or use a double-ended bath with central taps.
- **Keep the WC out of the door view.** Bathroom doors stay open; nobody should see the WC from the landing or, in an ensuite, from the bed. Place it around a corner or behind the door.
- **Use the stud walls**: cavities give niches, recessed mirrors, lighting and storage, not just a place for pipes. Adding a short or half wall can create better headroom for a shower or a clean end to a bath or vanity.
- **Shower niche not under the shower head**: the supply pipes run down that wall. Put niches on an adjacent or opposite wall unless the wall is deep enough.
- **Somewhere to put things by the basin**: an undermount basin or a shelf under the mirror.
- **A towel radiator** instead of a plain radiator.
- **Mirror cabinet depth vs basin depth**: a deep cabinet over a shallow basin hits your head when brushing teeth.
- **Sloped and low ceilings**: check head height where the shower goes, including the step up for the tray and drainage below.
- Swaps: an all-in-one WC and vanity unit for small rooms; a ceiling-mounted shower head for low ceilings (if the void allows pipes); a **back-to-wall WC instead of wall-hung on a stud wall** (the floor carries the weight).

**[YT-B2] Small-bathroom plans that work** (patterns only).
- **Linear**: everything on one wall, a straight clear path; suits long narrow rooms (about 1.5 × 2.4 m). A floating vanity and wall-hung WC show more floor.
- **Corner shower** with frameless glass and a sliding panel instead of a hinged door; suits square rooms.
- **Wet room**: no curb, one continuous floor falling to a linear drain; needs a full remodel and careful waterproofing.
- **Pocket door**, or an outward-opening door, to recover the swing space in very small rooms.
- **Recessed niches** between studs instead of shelves that stick out.
- **A raised shower zone** separates wet and dry without a wall.
- First view on opening the door should be the **vanity, not the WC**.

## Living rooms

**[YT-L1] An architect's eight-step method.**
1. **Map the desire paths** people will actually take before placing anything.
2. What's left are **pockets** where nobody walks: the biggest, quietest one takes the seating, the next the dining, slim edges a desk or shelves. People avoid sitting in traffic.
3. **Align to the room's axis** (window, fireplace, light point); start ordered and symmetric, then loosen.
4. **Frame the first view from the entrance**: a welcoming seating group, not the TV or the kitchen.
5. **Check what you look at from each seat**: a window or calm wall, not the messy kitchen or the WC door.
6. **A seating circle**: seats that face each other within conversation distance, with a mix of chair types; dining chairs can join when guests come.
7. **Anchor the middle** with a coffee table and side tables beside seats, which also gives each seat a sense of enclosure.
8. **Mark the boundary** with a rug that holds the group together.

**[YT-L2] Ten common mistakes.**
- **Sofa pushed flat against the wall** with an empty middle: pull it 20–30 cm off the wall, or float it with a console behind in larger rooms. `[std]`
- **Every seat facing the TV**: arrange faces first, screen second (a U or loose circle); check each seat can see the others without twisting.
- **No clear path**: a main walkway of about 0.9 m (agrees with the [NKBA] walkway the checker uses), about 0.36–0.46 m from sofa to coffee table (agrees with [RET-L]), and **the path goes around the conversation, not through it**.
- **A floating rug**: at least the front legs of every main seat on it; layer a small rug over a large plain one if needed.
- **TV too high**: centre of the screen near seated eye level; keep it as low as the mantel allows. A screen filling about 30° of view (SMPTE guidance) means a 50-inch TV is best within about 2.1 m.
- **Everything at one height**: mix tall (lamp, plant, bookcase) and low pieces.
- **All light from the ceiling**: layer lamps.

## Home office

**[YT-O1] Five mistakes.**
- Put the desk in the **command position**: a view of the door, a solid wall behind.
- **Never sit with your back to the door**; if facing it isn't possible, angle the desk across a corner for a partial view.
- **Separate work from home**: in open plans, define the work zone with a tall bookshelf, curtain or screen, and closed storage to put work away.
- In open plans with nothing behind you, add something solid at your back: a low bookcase, console or plants.
- **Three layers of light**: daylight (avoid windowless rooms), general light and a task light; cooler light for focus work, warmer for evenings.

## Utility and laundry

**[YT-U1] Washer and dishwasher in a small kitchen balcony** (about 2.4 × 1.2 m, door taking about 0.75 m of the length).
- A **dishwasher needs about 1.5 m in front** to open, load and stand, so in a 1.2 m-wide balcony it must face **along** the balcony, next to the washing machine, not across it. `[std]`
- **Measure the balcony door before buying**: here the opening was about 57–58 cm and the dishwasher 60 × 60 cm; it only went through with the top removed.
- Two machines in one place need **two sockets (15 A)**, a **two-way angle valve** for both water inlets, and **a drain standpipe each**.
- A utility balcony **next to the kitchen sink** makes loading the dishwasher easy.

## Small flats

**[YT-S1] Melbourne, 35 m²: office and home in one room.**
- Kept the walls and plumbing; spaces **appear and disappear**: a lounge chair converts to an office chair, folds away at night, and the TV is revealed.
- The dining table **slides out** only for guests (seats 4–6).
- A 4 m kitchen with 3 m of clear bench; everything concealed.
- An **internal window between bathroom and kitchen borrows daylight**, with switchable privacy glass.
- No outdoor space, so a preserved-moss green wall in the bathroom; mirrored cabinet doors and skirting make the room read bigger.

**[YT-S2] Paris, 60 m²: open plan with modules that close off.**
- Walls removed; living, kitchen and office in the centre, **bathroom and WC compact at the ends**. A **separate WC near the entrance** so guests don't enter the private rooms.
- Tall storage by the entry for coats and bike helmets; a long low unit for everything else.
- **One-wall kitchen** suits two people cooking together; integrated appliances; a recessed tiled dish-drying niche behind the sink.
- **Curtains instead of doors** to close the bedroom; a movable kitchen section hides a door so guests reach the bathroom without crossing the bedroom.
- A windowless bathroom **borrows light through a round window** from the bedroom.
- A **ceiling rope-and-pulley drying rack** in the bathroom's full-height section keeps laundry off the floor.
- A **two-desk office that converts to a guest room**, with a mezzanine bed reached by a retractable stair.

## Cross-cutting patterns (feed into the skill)

1. **Trace routes before placing furniture**, and keep them out of work zones and conversation groups [YT-K1, YT-L1, YT-L2].
2. **Draw every swing**: doors, appliance doors, drawers, the shower door [YT-K1, YT-B1, YT-B2, YT-U1].
3. **The first view matters**: from the front door, from the bathroom door (vanity, not WC) and from the bed [YT-B1, YT-B2, YT-L1].
4. **What you look at from where you spend time**: the sink, the sofa, the desk [YT-K1, YT-L1, YT-O1].
5. **Plan storage from a list** of what you own, in standard module widths [YT-K2], echoing the utensil list in [HT-I6].
6. **Services decide fixed points**: drains, stacks, ducts, sockets and valves [YT-K1, YT-K2, YT-U1].
7. **Borrowed light** through internal windows for windowless rooms [YT-S1, YT-S2].
8. **Rooms that convert**: office ↔ guest room, lounge ↔ office, dining on demand [YT-S1, YT-S2], like [HT-W1].
9. **Measure doors before buying appliances** [YT-U1].
