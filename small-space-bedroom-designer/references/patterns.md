# Archetype layouts

Use these as starting points, then adapt them to the real door and window positions. Areas and heights are the typical fit.

## A. Single child, study loft (6–9 m², ceiling ≥ 2.6)
Loft along the longest solid wall; desk and bookshelves under it; storage steps at the head end act as the wardrobe base; a full-height wardrobe on the opposite wall with sliding doors; centre kept open. Reveal: entry → loft → desk → steps → wardrobe.

## B. Single teen, gaming den (7–10 m², ceiling ≥ 2.5)
Loft over an enclosed den (sofa and screen, ≥ 1.0 clear) with a hidden door that looks like a wardrobe; desk in the window. Reveal: entry → "wardrobe" → secret den → loft → desk.

## C. Low-ceiling single (5–8 m², ceiling < 2.6)
Half-height bed (mattress top about 0.8) with drawers and a wardrobe under it; desk alongside under the window; niches replace nightstands.

## D. Twins, offset L-loft (9–11 m², ceiling ≥ 2.7)
Lower bed along one wall; upper bed perpendicular along the next wall, not overlapping, over a desk and wardrobe; storage steps at the junction; second desk in the window; mirrored wardrobe allocation. See `examples/twins-offset-loft.json`.

## E. Two kids, side-by-side platforms (≥ 3.2 m wide)
Two raised platforms (mattress top 0.5–0.8) against opposite walls or end to end, with a divider cabinet between them; wheeled drawers under; desks in a shared row at the window.

## F. Long room, bed on wardrobes (≥ 4 m long)
Back-to-back wardrobes forming a spine; the upper bed sits on top, reached by steps; lower bed by the window; each child's wardrobe faces their own side.

## G. 3+ kids, multi-bed steel system (≥ 12 m², ceiling ≥ 2.8)
A column-supported steel frame along two walls holding 3–6 beds; box-stairs as drawers; a long desk row; a drawer section per child; an open centre; protective ropes or rails on high steps.

## H. Couple in a small room (8–11 m²)
Platform bed with step drawers split his/hers; a walk-in closet behind a false wall at the bed head; a split WFH/vanity desk in the window; niches as nightstands.

## I. Micro-unit (≤ 4 m²)
A fold-up wall bed or a sofa-bed pulled from drawers; every wall full-height storage; a mirror to extend the space. Structural extensions only with an engineer.
